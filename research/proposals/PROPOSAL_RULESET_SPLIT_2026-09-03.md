CARTHOGRAPHER,

This is the Commander's "two rule sets" ruling, filed separately from the subordinate-office
amendment because it is a **constitutional migration**, not a clause. It re-homes the house's rules
into two books so that repository law and AI-conduct law get equal, unambiguous emphasis — instead of
being interleaved in one 618-line constitution where a cross-cutting rule can be "found" in the wrong
place and treated as gone.

---

# WHY THIS PROPOSAL EXISTS (summary)

The house keeps paying for one failure in different forms: **text says one thing, reality says
another, and the gap becomes a false citation downstream** — "Law XXXII," "Testament §7," and Seat R's
own misreading of `@the_forge` are all the same wound. A single interleaved lawbook makes that wound
worse, because a rule that spans both repositories and AI conduct can be cited in one place and
"missing" from the other. The Commander's ruling — two separate rule sets — fixes this by giving
repository law and AI law equal, fair emphasis. But a split constitution risks manufacturing the very
ambiguity it aims to cure, so this proposal ships with two non-negotiable load-bearing lines: the
**boundary rule** (conduct = AI, artifact = repository, both = both) and the **Rule Index** (no rule
is citable unless it is in the index). The index turns "two books" from a search problem into a
lookup, and gives the house's machine checks a single surface to lint. This is a migration, filed
separately because re-homing every rule deserves its own watch — not a side-clause of the
subordinates' ratification.

---

# 1. THE SPLIT

| Book | Holds | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Repository Rules** | Law about the *artifact*: files, branches, packaging, versioning, history, releases | Commandments I/III/VII/VIII, Law VIII (patch packaging), Law XV-A (delivery), merge/gate rules |
| **AI Rules** | Law about the *conduct*: honesty, verification, succession, seat authority, non-destruction | Law X (no false completion), Law I (non-destructive), Law XVII/XVII-A/B/C/D (succession), Law XIX-B (Scout's Voice), Commandments II/VI/X |

# 2. THE BOUNDARY RULE (the load-bearing line)

> **"A rule governing an AI's conduct is an AI rule; a rule governing the repository is a repository
> rule; a rule that governs both appears in both."**

This is non-negotiable. Law X (no false completion) is an AI rule *and* a repository-claim rule.
Commandment I (packaging) is both. Without the boundary line, a split constitution manufactures
exactly the ambiguity it exists to kill — a rule cited in one book and "missing" from the other is
the "Law XXXII" failure, structuralized.

# 3. THE INDEX (the second load-bearing line)

> **"A single Rule Index maps every rule to its book(s). No rule may be cited, enforced, or amended
> unless it appears in the index. The index is the source of truth for *where a rule lives*, and is
> itself governed as a repository rule."**

The index is what lets the split ship safely: it converts "two books" from a search problem into a
lookup. It also gives the machine checks the house already favors (`governance-audit.js`,
`scout-voice-check.js`) a single, verifiable surface to lint against.

# 4. MIGRATION — WHAT MOVES, WHAT DOESN'T

- **Do not rewrite rule text.** Migration re-homes; it does not re-word. Re-wording during a move is
  how rules silently change meaning (Commandment III — preservation of provenance).
- **Carry the Amendment Ledger with it.** Lettered amendments (XIII-A, XV-A, XVII-A/B/C/D, XVIII-A/B,
  XIX-A/B/C) stay lettered and stay with their parent law — the ledger is the continuity instrument.
- **Commandments follow the same split** (I/VII/VIII → repository; II/VI/X → AI; III spans both).
- **Do not split a rule mid-clause.** A law moves whole; if it spans both books, it appears in both
  *verbatim*, and the index records both locations.

# 5. RISKS & GUARDS

| Risk | Guard |
|---|---|
| A cross-cutting rule is "missing" from one book and treated as gone | Boundary rule + index (above) |
| Migration rewrites meaning | "Re-home, never re-word" + Commandment III |
| The split adds lookup friction that discourages reading | One index; machine-checkable (the house's own preference) |
| Two books drift apart | Index is linted by `governance-audit.js`; drift fails the check |

# 6. DISPOSITION REQUESTED

Class D — Commander ratification + Joint assent. Recommend the index and boundary rule be drafted
*before* the migration begins, so the move is mechanical, not editorial.
